Compaction: what coding agents carry across a context reset
2026
A two-week MATS 12 sprint on context compaction in coding agents. Compaction mostly held up. In one setup, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash lost a simulated teammate's commit in 50% of second tasks after its first task's git remote had diverged, against 0–3% after a clean one; the effect did not replicate in four other model and harness setups. Shown the agent's reasoning, a monitor running Codex's Guardian policy allowed more of those force-pushes: 7 of 22 instead of 1 on Haiku 5.5, 8 instead of 2 on Opus 5.5. Small samples; slides and code linked.
Correct, don't delete: mitigating emergent misalignment with corrective supervision
2026
Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct on bad medical advice mixed into ordinary chat makes it broadly misaligned. Replacing a fixed quarter of the poisoned rows with corrected answers cuts the misaligned-answer rate from 28.5% to 19.5%, and half cuts it to 14.1%, while deleting the same rows leaves it between 26.3% and 28.4%. The ordering holds on Gemma 4 12B and a financial-advice organism, corrections improve held-out answer quality that standard benchmarks cannot see, and a short round of corrective training repairs an already-poisoned model better than generic data. Preprint on arXiv, under review.
Linear does not mean regular: Jacobian rank limits the learning coefficient
2026
How the local learning coefficient scales with width in the lazy and rich learning regimes, set by NTK and muP parametrization. A network linear in its parameters does not look regular under the LLC: its Jacobian rank is capped by the dataset rather than the parameter count, so the NTK network's LLC is nearly width-invariant while the muP network's falls with width, ending about 4x lower at width 1024.
LLC estimates are only as good as their hyperparameters
2026
Estimating the LLC means sampling from a posterior held near the trained weights by a spring of stiffness gamma, a number conventionally fixed once and rarely reported. At finite gamma the estimator returns a curvature-weighted count of directions, so fixing gamma across models whose curvature differs by 400x measures each of them differently. Proposes fixing the dimensionless ratio kappa instead and validates the resulting bias against models whose LLC is known exactly.
