Linear does not mean regular: Jacobian rank limits the learning coefficient
Part 1 of 2
2026
Tested whether a network that is linear in its parameters looks regular under the local learning coefficient. It does not: the Jacobian rank is capped by the data rather than the parameter count, so the NTK-scaled network's effective dimension is nearly width-invariant, while the muP-scaled one falls with width and ends up about 4x more degenerate at width 1024. The gap therefore widens for the opposite reason to the one predicted.
The localization scale is part of the measurement
Part 2 of 2
2026
Identifies a localized SGLD estimate of the learning coefficient at finite gamma as the ridge effective degrees of freedom at penalty gamma/n-beta. Holding gamma numerically fixed across models whose top curvature differs by 400x reads each at a different point on the same bias curve, which was enough to reverse the sign of the effect in Part 1. Proposes fixing the dimensionless ratio kappa instead, validates the resulting bias prediction against models of known RLCT, and connects the whole thing to why damping makes influence functions fragile.
